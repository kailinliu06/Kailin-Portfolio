Making a Technical B2B Product Easier to Find, Understand, and Buy

During a two-month internship at a biotechnology manufacturer, I worked across B2B marketplace listings, buyer-facing content, sales materials, and prospect outreach — translating technical product information into clearer messaging and supporting international lead generation.

01Marketing a Technical B2B Product

Cardlo manufactures food emulsifiers and plastic additives for international B2B buyers. Unlike consumer products, these materials are evaluated heavily on application fit, reliability, and performance.

Cardlo already sold internationally and had listings on B2B marketplaces, but its buyer-facing content and overseas marketing presence were still limited.

My task: learn the product quickly, improve how prospective buyers discovered and understood it, and support international lead generation through targeted outreach.

02Translating Features Into Buyer Value

I started by learning how Glycerin Monostearate was used across food, cosmetics, and plastics manufacturing, and which product attributes mattered most in those applications.

The next step was translating those technical features into the concerns a buyer would have when evaluating the material:

FeatureBuyer ConcernBuyer Value
Monoglyceride content >99%Can we rely on the material for consistent quality in production?More confidence in product quality and fewer performance uncertainties
Emulsification & stabilityWill it perform reliably in our specific application?More stable processing and more predictable end-product performance
I used this buyer-first framing across listings, outreach, and sales videos — starting with what a buyer needed to evaluate, then choosing the technical details and applications that made the product easier to trust and assess.

03Improving Discovery Across B2B Channels

Cardlo already had listings on Alibaba International and YiXuan, so I focused on improving how buyers found and evaluated the products through those pages.

  • Ran keyword research and competitor benchmarking to understand how comparable products were being found and positioned
  • Reworked product titles and descriptions to make key applications and buyer value clearer
  • Adjusted pricing presentation and product images based on how competitors positioned similar materials
  • Tracked page views and inquiries to see which changes were improving discovery and interest
Listing page views increased by roughly 35%, alongside an increase in customer inquiries.

I also launched the company's first Twitter presence, adapting product and industry content for an overseas audience.

04Sales Enablement & Outreach

To support sales conversations, I created clearer materials around product applications and company capabilities, then paired that content with targeted prospect research and outreach.

  • Sales content: Planned, filmed, and edited five product and company videos covering application use cases and company capabilities, giving the sales team reusable materials for prospect conversations
  • Prospect research: Sourced potential buyers through B2B marketplaces, Google, and industry sources, then prioritized them by market, company profile, and product fit
  • Outreach: Sent 100+ personalized prospecting emails based on each buyer's likely needs and followed up systematically, generating 10+ positive prospect responses

I also supported later-stage sales activities, including order processing and export documentation, which gave me visibility into the broader B2B sales cycle.

05Results & Key Learning

Over two months, I helped improve how Cardlo's products were presented, discovered, and used in sales conversations — increasing listing page views by roughly 35%, generating more inquiries, earning 10+ positive responses from outbound outreach, and creating reusable content for the sales team.

The experience taught me that marketing a technical B2B product starts with understanding what the buyer needs to know and translating features into clear business value. I also learned that, for experienced B2B buyers, credibility often comes from specificity — clear technical details, relevant applications, and enough evidence to judge whether the product fits their needs.
