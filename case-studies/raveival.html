01Validating an Early-Stage Marketplace

Raveival was an early-stage festival-fashion resale marketplace, where users could buy and sell outfits for festivals and events. At the time, the product was still taking shape, with open questions around target users, unmet needs, UX, positioning, and real market demand.

My role: conduct broad early-stage research across customer discovery, market and competitor analysis, UX, business-model exploration, and field testing to help the team make more informed product and go-to-market decisions.

02Who Were We Actually Building For?

Before testing growth, I helped shape the team's customer research approach to turn a broad idea of "festival-goers" into a segment we could actually design and market for.

  • Research design: combined survey data, 1:1 interviews, and small focus groups to understand purchase drivers, unmet needs, and how shopping behavior differed across user groups
  • Segmentation: segmented responses by age, gender, and festival frequency to move beyond the broad "festival-goer" label and identify patterns tied to actual buying behavior
  • Target signal: identified the clearest early signal among women aged 22–25 — frequent festival-goers who valued distinctive outfits and showed stronger openness to secondhand fashion
  • Qualitative insight: used interviews to explain the "why" behind those patterns, especially around uniqueness, shopping friction, and dissatisfaction with existing options
What changed: the research narrowed a broad audience assumption into a more specific target segment, giving the team a clearer basis for positioning and what to test next.

03Finding Room in a Crowded Market

Customer interest mattered only if Raveival could occupy a meaningful position in an already crowded festival-fashion market. I structured the research around industry context, competitor benchmarking, and product positioning.

  • Industry scan: reviewed industry reports, company materials, and financial information to understand broader market dynamics and how established players were evolving
  • Competitor benchmarking: compared 5–8 leading competitors across positioning, pricing, target customers, assortment, and business model using a benchmarking matrix and competitive positioning map to understand how brands differentiated and where they clustered
  • Product structure: analyzed how competitors organized and described their assortments, using category architecture and product language as references for Raveival's own marketplace
  • Strategic application: brought the research into early discussions around positioning, assortment, pricing, channel selection, and market-entry direction
What changed: the research gave the team a clearer view of how the market was structured, where Raveival could fit, and which product and go-to-market decisions needed further testing.

04Would Interest Turn Into Early Demand?

Before launch, we needed a way to test whether the concept could attract real interest beyond surveys. I helped turn that question into a series of low-cost, in-person pre-launch activations focused on waitlist sign-ups and live feedback.

  • Location strategy: selected campus events and Deltopia based on audience fit, focusing on places where likely festival-goers were already gathering
  • Activation design: created simple booth materials and low-cost engagement mechanics, including temporary tattoos, to attract attention and open conversations around the concept
  • Pitch & feedback system: developed a repeatable pitch and lightweight feedback process using paper forms and Google Forms to capture reactions, questions, and sign-ups consistently
  • Pre-launch validation: used the activations to test whether people would move from passive interest to a concrete next step by joining the waitlist
400+pre-launch waitlist sign-ups
from 2 in-person activations in one month

The activations gave the team its first meaningful pre-launch demand signal — showing that the concept could attract enough interest for people to engage and join the waitlist before the product was live.

05Turning Early Signals Into Business Decisions

As the team moved from exploration toward sharper decisions, I helped synthesize customer research, market findings, and pre-launch signals into the next round of product and business choices.

  • Business model: helped refine the Business Model Canvas, using customer and market research to clarify target segments, value proposition, channels, customer relationships, and revenue assumptions
  • Pricing & monetization: brought competitor and customer insights into pricing discussions and proposed a tiered pricing direction to accommodate different customer needs and willingness to pay
  • Go-to-market direction: used market research and activation feedback to support early decisions around acquisition channels and where the team should focus further testing
  • Ongoing strategy: shared research findings and recommendations in weekly discussions with the founder, helping connect individual research projects to broader product and business questions
Broad assumptions → clearer customer → early demand signal → more informed business direction.
