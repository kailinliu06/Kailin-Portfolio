I supported YouTube influencer marketing across CocoSoft's AI-product portfolio, spanning creator research, segmentation, negotiation, content briefing, and performance optimization. 01Earning Ownership Before Launch

I joined CocoSoft supporting established AI products including BypassGPT and EssayGPT, where I learned the team's existing creator marketing process. When the company began preparing LoudMe.ai, a new AI music-generation product, I became interested before its public launch and started exploring it independently.

Before formal outreach began, I:

  • Researched how adjacent AI and music products were being positioned and promoted
  • Mapped the relevant creator landscape and identified potential partners
  • Explored which creator types could best communicate different product use cases
  • Drafted the campaign brief myself — work that would normally have been led by my mentor
That initiative led to substantial ownership of LoudMe.ai's YouTube creator campaign, from early creator strategy and selection through negotiation, content review, launch, and performance tracking.

02Building the Creator Intelligence Library

Rather than treating sourcing as one-off outreach, I built a structured creator library to compare opportunities across audience fit, content style, pricing, and performance. For each creator, I tracked factors including:

  • Creator/channel type and content style
  • Audience size and geography
  • Historical view benchmarks and engagement quality
  • Recent view performance and publishing cadence
  • Estimated CPM based on prior performance
  • Single-video and package pricing
  • Product fit and campaign performance

The library gave me a consistent way to compare creators across the same dimensions instead of evaluating each opportunity in isolation. It also remained a reusable team resource after my internship, helping preserve partner history and inform future outreach.

03Matching Creator Types to Marketing Roles

I stopped screening creators by reach alone. For an unfamiliar AI product, different creator types could serve very different marketing purposes, so I began mapping creator type to the role they could play before outreach. LoudMe.ai is one example of how I applied that approach.

Creator TypeRolePurpose
AI reviewersProduct education / technical credibilityExplain what the technology does and why it's different

Vocal / music creatorsProduct demonstrationMake output quality and creative possibilities tangible

This changed how I thought about creator value. The more useful question was what job each creator could perform for the product: explain it, validate a use case, demonstrate the output, or make the technology feel more accessible.

Across the AI-product portfolio, I sourced and reached out to hundreds of creators, ultimately securing 70+ creator partnerships. My role covered the full partnership process, from outreach and negotiation to briefing, content review, and launch.

  • Negotiation: I asked creators for both a single-video rate and a multi-video package rate. The package quote helped me understand how much flexibility they had on pricing. I then looked at their recent views, past performance, content quality, and estimated CPM to decide what a reasonable first-video rate should be.
  • Partnership decisions: We usually started with one video. After it went live, I looked at content quality, product fit, traffic, registrations, and overall performance before deciding whether it made sense to work with the creator again.
  • Campaign briefing: I wrote briefs covering the key product message, features, suggested filming angles, and a rough script structure, while giving creators room to adapt the video to their own content style.
  • Content review and launch: I reviewed videos before publication, checked product and brand accuracy, refined titles and descriptions for search, and coordinated publishing timing with creators.

05Optimizing for Downstream Performance

Using Google Analytics, YouTube Analytics, and Excel, I compared creator-level traffic, CTR, CPM, registrations, and conversion, then looked at those results alongside audience fit, recent traffic, and content quality.

This helped me understand which partnerships were actually driving useful acquisition, and shaped future decisions on who to re-engage, which creator types to prioritize, and where to allocate budget.

06Results & Key Learning

Across the AI-product portfolio I supported, the creator program generated:

  • 650,000+ cumulative views
  • 2,500+ product registrations
  • CPM below $15
The biggest takeaway was that reach alone did not tell me which partnerships were most valuable. Creators performed best when their audience, content style, and role matched what the product needed to communicate — and when that fit carried through to traffic and registrations.

07Lasting Organic Value

Nearly two years after the campaign, one LoudMe.ai creator I personally sourced, vetted, negotiated with, and launched still ranks among the top five YouTube search results for "loudme.ai."

The placement continued to generate organic product discovery long after the original collaboration ended.
