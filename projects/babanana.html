Production & Project Management · Not a Marketing Case Study

Building an operations framework from zero, twice — for a 36-person live production

Producer / Co-Director / Music Director for two original Chinese-language musicals at UC Santa Barbara. No marketing angle here — this is the clearest evidence I have of running a real project: budget, timeline, cross-functional recruiting, and a room full of people depending on the schedule holding.

01Year One — Building From Zero

A friend and I co-founded and produced the club's first original Chinese-language musical, splitting the entire production between two people — with no existing playbook to follow.

  • Roles covered between the two of us: producer, assistant director, music director (music, sound, vocal coaching), costume design, stage management
  • Designed the casting process from scratch — recruitment posting, audition format, vocal testing — then managed a 25+ person cast and crew through it
  • Wrote the script, selected and arranged 10+ songs, ran rehearsals, coordinated venue booking, and handled costumes and props
  • Negotiated directly with the school's financial and administrative offices to secure funding, raising about $2,500

We delivered a 1.5-hour show to 100+ audience members — a real, ticketed production, run by two students with no institutional template to follow.

02Year Two — Scaling Up: OUT: An Asian American Musical

The second year scaled the operation significantly: 36 cast and crew, a 2.5-hour show (up from 1.5), and a live band for the first time. As Producer and Music Director, I owned the full production timeline, from recruitment and auditions through three live performances.

  • Recruited across departments and outside organizations — the music department, the university orchestra, other theater groups — and scouted cast/musicians via social media and live performances, to fill a limited campus talent pool
  • Handled song arrangement, orchestration, vocal part assignment, and ongoing rehearsal coordination between the cast and the band
  • Refined the rehearsal schedule and workflow as the group grew, resolved cross-department coordination problems, and acted as the central point between music, performance, promotion, costume, and live logistics
Negotiating licensing directly with the original composer team was its own workstream — rights compliance, cross-team communication, and relationship management, on top of running rehearsals.

We raised $6,000+ — more than double Year One — and closed with three sold-out performances totaling 400+ attendees.

03What This Actually Required

Strip away the theater context and this was running a small, time-boxed organization twice — the second time at nearly 1.5x the scale — with real constraints: a fixed performance date that couldn't move, a budget that had to be negotiated from scratch each year, and a team built almost entirely from cold outreach across departments I had no formal authority in.

  • Designed hiring/casting processes and ran them at 25- and then 36-person scale
  • Owned budgeting and funding negotiation with school administration, doubling the raised amount year over year
  • Recruited cross-functionally across departments and outside organizations to fill specialized gaps (musicians, vocalists)
  • Negotiated licensing and managed an external stakeholder relationship (the original composer team)
  • Built and maintained a rehearsal schedule and workflow across a growing team, resolving coordination conflicts under a fixed deadline

It's not a marketing project, and I'm not framing it as one. It's the clearest single answer I have to "can you actually run something end to end, under real constraints, with people who don't report to you" — which is why it's here.
